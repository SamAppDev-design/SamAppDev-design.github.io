Reflection — What I Learned from the Sprint Backlog

Sprint 2 was the most intense and most educational sprint of the GoShuttle project. The sprint backlog made the abstract feel concrete — instead of "we are building a shuttle app," we had 20 specific tasks, each with an owner, an estimate and a deadline window. The backlog turned a month of work into a set of daily decisions.

As Team Leader, I learned that my job during the sprint was not to do all the work — it was to unblock my team. When Mvuyisi could not start Package 3 until Sinethemba's Route table was seeded (T-07 through T-11), I tracked that dependency daily. The moment T-11 was done, I notified Mvuyisi immediately so he could start T-12 the same day. This "dependency tracking" role is something I had never considered part of leadership before this sprint.

The sprint retrospective revealed our estimation blind spot. T-17 (PassService) was estimated at 4 points but clearly needed 6. Going forward, I will apply the Agile rule of thumb: if a task involves both backend logic and frontend display, add 2 points to whatever you first estimate. The cognitive overhead of connecting two layers is always underestimated.

Most importantly, Sprint 2 showed me what "done" really means in Agile. T-14 (TrackView.vue) was not done when the map appeared on screen — it was done when it polled Supabase every 10 seconds, drew the route line, and positioned the bus icon correctly. A task is done when it meets the acceptance criteria of its user story, not when the developer thinks it looks finished. That distinction will shape how I define completion on every future project.
